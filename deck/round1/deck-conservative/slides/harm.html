A cloned voice on the helpline

Before the call

The fraudster takes a short voice sample from a public video or voice note, and personal details from a leaked database.

The call

They ring the helpline as the account holder, answer the identity questions and speak in the cloned voice.

The approval

Voice ID, or the agent’s ear, accepts the voice. The answers are correct, so a limit change, new payee or SIM-linked change goes through.

After the call

The money moves. The customer finds out from an SMS or a failed payment.

All cyber fraud, not only voice

One precedent

In 2023 a reporter used an AI clone of his own voice to pass his UK bank’s voice ID and reach his account (Vice).

Primary user

Helpline agent and the fraud-ops analyst behind them

Protected

The account holder whose voice is cloned

In scope

Helpline calls at banks that use or add voice ID, as ICICI did in 2015

Not in scope

Calls to relatives, video deepfakes, SIM-swap itself

Say: the harm happens in the approval step. The agent trusts a voice and correct answers, and both can now be faked. Figures: Rs 22,845.73 crore lost in 2024 and Rs 7,465.18 crore in 2023, with 22,68,346 cybercrime cases on NCRP in 2024, from a written reply by MoS Home Bandi Sanjay Kumar in the Lok Sabha on 22 Jul 2025, as reported by PTI (The Week) and Scroll. The share caused by voice cloning is not published, so we do not claim one. Precedent: Joseph Cox, Vice Motherboard, Feb 2023, used a free AI voice clone to pass Lloyds Bank voice ID on his own account. Context only, not on this page: McAfee (May 2023) found 47% of Indian respondents had met an AI voice scam or knew a victim; that survey is mostly about calls to family, which is out of our scope. Why voice matters at all: agents rarely know a customer's voice, so the risk is real where banks use voice ID or where relationship staff know the caller.
