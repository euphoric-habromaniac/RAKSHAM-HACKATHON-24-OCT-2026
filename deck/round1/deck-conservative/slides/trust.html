Trust: five questions from the brief

Designed, not yet tested

What data do we need?

Live caller audio and its transcript, a consented voice embedding, and bank flags: new device, new payee, amount jump. Not collected: account numbers or balances; no audio or transcript kept unless a case is held.

What if the system is wrong?

Nothing is blocked on one weak signal. A false alarm costs the customer one extra step. A miss may be caught by post-call review and the bank’s payee and limit controls. Overrides are logged with a reason.

Who can act on the result?

The agent sees a band and a message, not raw scores. Policy can pause a request; only an analyst can release or escalate it. Account actions stay with the bank’s fraud process.

How is the result explained?

A plain message with the next step, such as “Voice differs from the enrolled sample. Ask the customer to confirm in the app.” A score alone is never shown as proof.

How does it resist misuse?

Digits are random; attempts are capped per account and per call. The caller learns only that an extra proof was asked, never which check fired or the score. Evidence is role-gated and logged. Voiceprints cover consenting customers only.

Another way in. A customer who declines voice enrolment verifies in the app only. Voice is never the only way to verify.

Fairness. Step-up and Hold rates for genuine callers by accent group, including digit errors on Hindi numerals. Allowed gap: [x] points.
